CAMP-03 — Academic Structure & Student Lifecycle Architecture
Architecture specification v1.0
 India-first
 Foundation domain


Our next major foundation is the academic world itself.

CAMP-01 tells us what the institution looks like. CAMP-02 tells us who operates within it. CAMP-03 will tell us what an educational institution teaches, how students progress through it, and how their academic history is maintained throughout their educational journey.

I want to make one major architectural decision upfront:

We will not build a B.Tech-specific academic database. We will build a configurable academic system capable of representing B.Tech, BCA, BBA, MBA, diploma, postgraduate, doctoral, vocational and other educational structures.

A B.Tech CSE student is one possible configuration, not the data model.
1. The academic architecture: six interconnected domains
I recommend separating CAMP-03 into six areas.
Academic operating model
A. Academic Structure

Schools, academic departments, programs, qualifications, academic units and delivery structures.

B. Curriculum & Course Management

Curriculum versions, subjects, courses, electives, prerequisites, credits and learning outcomes.

C. Academic Calendar & Term Management

Academic years, semesters, trimesters, examination periods, registration windows and academic events.

D. Student Lifecycle & Enrollment

Admission handoff, enrollment, program changes, course registration, progression, leave, withdrawal and graduation.

E. Academic Records & Credit Management

Grades, credits, attempts, transcripts, academic standing, transfer credits and historical academic records.

F. Academic Rules & Progression

Prerequisites, course selection, credit requirements, promotion, repeat rules, academic equivalence and graduation eligibility.


These are logical subdomains, not necessarily six separately deployed applications.
2. Important ownership boundary
We need to avoid creating overlaps with CAMP-01, CAMP-02 and future domains.
Domain	Owns
CAMP-01	Institution, campus, organizational and academic-unit identity
CAMP-02	Person, account, membership and authorization
CAMP-03	Programs, curricula, academic enrollment and progression
CAMP-07	Attendance records and attendance processing
CAMP-08	Examination administration, assessments, result evaluation and publication
CAMP-09	Placement and recruitment eligibility
CAMP-18	Institutional governance, approval and compliance

There is an important distinction between academic records and examination operations.

For example:
- CAMP-08 conducts an examination.
- CAMP-08 evaluates and publishes results.
- CAMP-03 receives the authoritative published outcome.
- CAMP-03 updates the student's academic history and credit ledger.
- CAMP-03 evaluates academic progression.
- CAMP-09 consumes authoritative academic facts to determine placement eligibility.
This prevents examination, academic progression and placement logic from being duplicated.

3. India-first academic flexibility
Before designing entities, we must understand the academic systems the platform must represent.

India's higher-education environment includes different credit structures, academic calendars, degree models and progression mechanisms.

The UGC's undergraduate Curriculum and Credit Framework supports flexible credit-based study, multidisciplinary learning and multiple entry/exit options. The Academic Bank of Credits provides a mechanism for credit accumulation, recognition, transfer and redemption. The National Credit Framework also discusses academic, vocational and experiential learning. 
UGC+2


These are particularly important to our design because we cannot assume that every student completes a fixed sequence of subjects at one institution.

The UGC's regulations catalogue also includes newer regulatory instruments, including the 2025 Minimum Standards and Procedure for Award of Undergraduate Degree and Postgraduate Degree Regulations. Our regulatory configuration therefore needs to be versioned rather than permanently tied to a particular framework. 
UGC

3.1 Academic models we must support
Academic model	Example
Annual	Year 1, Year 2, Year 3
Semester	Semester 1 through 8
Trimester	Three academic terms per year
Quarter	Four academic periods

Modular	Independent learning modules
Competency-based	Progression through demonstrated competencies
Research-based	Research milestones and thesis progression

An institution can even operate several models simultaneously.

For example, B.Tech might use semesters, MBA might use trimesters, and a diploma program might use annual progression.

Therefore, the academic calendar cannot be a global singleton with eight semesters hardcoded into it.

4. Academic structure — the hierarchy of education
Now we start defining actual entities.

The first thing to understand is that an institution's organizational hierarchy and its academic structure are related but different.

CAMP-01 provides the academic-unit identities. CAMP-03 defines the educational programs and academic offerings operating within them.

The program itself should not be confused with the department delivering it.

A program can be jointly delivered by multiple academic units, and the department responsible for administration can change.

4.3 Qualification
This represents the academic credential a program is designed to award.

Examples:
- Bachelor of Technology.
- Bachelor of Computer Applications.
- Master of Business Administration.
- Diploma.
- Postgraduate Diploma.
- PhD.

Do not hardcode a qualification solely as a string field inside a program.

Multiple programs can lead to the same qualification, and one program may support different approved exit awards.
4.4 Program versus Program Offering
This distinction is essential.

Program: The academic definition of an educational pathway.

Example: B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering.

Program Offering: An actual instance of that program delivered by an institution at a particular campus, through a particular delivery mode and admission cycle.

Example:
Program:
    B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering

Program Offering A:
    Campus North
    Regular
    Admission cycle 2026
    Active

Program Offering B:
    Campus South
    Evening
    Admission cycle 2026
    Active


Recommended entities:
Entity	Responsibility
Program	Program identity
ProgramVersion	Versioned academic definition
ProgramOffering	Delivery instance
ProgramOfferingUnit	Academic units responsible for delivery
ProgramQualification	Qualification associated with the program
ProgramSpecialization	Optional specialization definition
ProgramAccreditationReference	Relevant external reference

A program should not be duplicated simply because it is available at another campus.
4.5 Program specialization
We need to support:
- Computer Science and Engineering.
- CSE with AI & ML specialization.
- CSE with Data Science specialization.
- CSE with Cybersecurity specialization.
But specialization can appear in different ways depending on institutional curriculum design.

Sometimes it is a separate program.

Sometimes it is a specialization within the same program.

Sometimes it is simply a student-selectable elective pathway.

Therefore, we should support both:
- Independently defined programs.
- Versioned specialization pathways within programs.
Do not assume every specialization has the same effect on the degree title.

5. Curriculum architecture — the heart of the academic system
This is where we need to be particularly careful.

A common database design would look like this:
Program
    |
    +-- Semester 1
    |       +-- Mathematics
    |       +-- Physics
    |
    +-- Semester 2
            +-- Programming
            +-- Chemistry


This is insufficient.

Why?

Because curriculum changes over time, students from different admission batches may follow different curricula, courses may be shared across programs, electives can be selected dynamically, and students can transfer credits.

We need an independently versioned curriculum model.

Once published and used for enrollment, a curriculum version should be immutable.

Changes should create a new version or a controlled amendment.

We must preserve the curriculum under which a student entered the program, while allowing explicitly approved migration to a different version.
5.3 Course catalogue
We need a reusable course catalogue.

Entity: Course.

Examples:
- Engineering Mathematics I.
- Physics.
- Programming Fundamentals.
- Data Structures and Algorithms.
- Database Management Systems.
- Machine Learning.
A course should have a stable institutional identity, separate from its specific syllabus version.

CourseVersion should contain:

We need to distinguish a course from its offering.

A course definition may exist for many years, while an actual course offering runs in a particular term.
5.4 Course categories
We should not hardcode only theory and lab.

A flexible classification model can include:
Category	Examples
Core	Mandatory program subjects

Open elective	Cross-disciplinary courses
Minor	Courses for a minor pathway

Project	Capstone and project work
Research	Research and thesis
Audit	Non-credit learning
Mandatory activity	Institution-required activity

Use configurable classification codes and credit-bearing flags.

Course categories can also have multiple labels where appropriate.

For example, a course may be both an open elective and a skill-oriented course.
5.5 Curriculum requirement groups
One of the most important entities should be CurriculumRequirementGroup.

A student does not always have to take the exact same list of courses.

Consider:
Program Version
    |
    +-- Mandatory Mathematics (4 credits)
    |
    +-- Mandatory Programming (4 credits)
    |
    +-- Choose ANY 2 from:
    |       +-- Machine Learning
    |       +-- Computer Vision
    |       +-- NLP
    |       +-- Data Mining
    |
    +-- Complete 8 credits of Open Electives
    |
    +-- Internship requirement
    |
    +-- Capstone project


We need to represent:
- Required courses.
- Minimum courses from a pool.
- Maximum courses from a pool.
- Minimum and maximum credit totals.
- Alternative course groups.
- Prerequisite requirements.
- Learning outcome requirements.
- Mandatory non-credit activities.
Recommended entities:
Entity	Purpose
CurriculumRequirementGroup	Requirement group

CurriculumCreditRequirement	Credit thresholds
CurriculumChoiceRule	Choose N courses or credits

This allows us to model substantially different curricula without creating custom tables per institution.
5.6 Course prerequisites and co-requisites
We need to support more than a simple prerequisite string.

Examples:
- Must pass Mathematics I before Mathematics II.
- Must complete Data Structures before Algorithms.
- Must have registered for a lab alongside its associated theory course.
- Must have completed a specified number of credits before selecting an advanced elective.
A prerequisite can be a structured expression.
AND
    |
    +-- Passed Course: CS201
    |
    +-- Completed Credits >= 40
    |
    +-- Academic Standing = GOOD


We should support:
- Course prerequisite.
- Course co-requisite.
- Credit threshold.
- Minimum grade.
- Program-specific restriction.
- Completed learning outcome.
- Approved waiver.
The evaluator must understand that merely having attempted a prerequisite does not necessarily mean it has been passed.

Approved waivers should also be auditable and should not change the student's historical grade.

6. Credit architecture — a particularly important decision
We must design our credit model carefully because it affects curricula, student mobility, progression and future job intelligence.

First, distinguish:
- Credits assigned to a course.
- Credits registered.
- Credits attempted.
- Credits awarded.
- Credits transferred.
- Credits recognized.
- Credits redeemed against a qualification requirement.
These are different concepts.

For example, a student registering for a four-credit course has not yet earned four credits.
6.1 Credit types
Type	Description
Academic credit	Credit earned through academic study
Vocational credit	Credit from approved vocational learning
Experiential credit	Credit awarded for approved experiential learning
Transfer credit	Credit recognized from another institution
Prior-learning credit	Credit recognized through an approved assessment
Non-credit	Learning with no credit allocation

The system should allow institutions to define how each credit category is treated.

The National Credit Framework discusses creditization and the integration of academic, vocational and experiential learning. The model should therefore be extensible to such credit sources, while retaining the authority and evidence behind each award. 
UGC+1

6.2 Never store only a current credit total
Suppose a student has completed 72 credits.

We need to know:
- Which courses generated those credits?
- Which were transferred?
- Which were earned through prior learning?
- Which were recognized through equivalence?
- Which program requirements did they satisfy?
- Were any credits later corrected?
- Were any credits excluded from a particular award?
A single field such as student.total_credits = 72 loses all of that history.

Instead, use a credit ledger.
6.3 Academic credit ledger
AcademicCreditEntry
Field	Purpose
credit_entry_id	Entry ID
student_enrollment_id	Student's program enrollment
source_type	Course, transfer, prior learning, etc.
source_record_id	Originating academic record
credit_amount	Awarded quantity
credit_type	Academic, vocational, experiential
awarding_authority	Institution or authorized source
effective_date	When credit is effective
recognition_status	Pending, recognized, rejected
evidence_reference	Supporting evidence
correction_reference	Reversal or adjustment link

The ledger should be append-only from a business perspective.

If a credit is awarded incorrectly, don't silently edit an old entry.

Instead, record a reversal or adjustment referencing the original transaction, with the required approvals.
6.4 Course-result and credit-award relationship
Course Registration
        |
Assessment and evaluation
        |
Published result
        |
Pass/fail decision
        |
Credit award policy
        |
Academic credit ledger entry
        |
Program requirement evaluation


The result is owned by CAMP-08. The credit award follows the institution's approved academic policy.

A course can have different outcomes:
- Passed and awarded credits.
- Failed with no credits.
- Passed after a repeat attempt.
- Exempted.
- Recognized through equivalence.
- Transferred from another institution.
We must not automatically treat every positive grade as a credit award without the applicable policy.
6.5 Academic Bank of Credits integration
We should build an AcademicCreditIntegration adapter.

It should eventually support:
- Student academic account references.
- Institution participation and registration.
- Published credit transmission.
- Credit verification.
- External credit recognition.
- Transfer requests.
- Credit reconciliation.
- Audit of external acknowledgements.
The UGC Academic Bank of Credits materials make an important distinction: credits are transmitted by the respective registered institution rather than accepted as authoritative documents directly from the student. This is exactly why our credit ledger needs issuer identity and evidence provenance. 
Compendium of UGC Regulations 1957-2023+1


Our internal credit ledger is the authoritative institutional record for the data our institution owns. ABC remains an external national integration and should not be recreated as our own national credential authority.

7.6 Time-dependent policies
An important rule:

Every academic calendar and progression policy should be evaluated against the correct effective academic period.

Suppose a college changes the registration deadline.

Students who registered under the old approved calendar should retain a historical record of the applicable deadline.

Don't retroactively modify old academic events by editing calendar dates.

Create new versions or explicit amendments.


8. Student lifecycle architecture
Now we get to one of the largest parts of the campus system.

We must model the student's complete institutional academic journey.

From the moment an admission decision is accepted, through enrollment, semester registration, academic progression, transfers, backlogs, leaves, re-entry and graduation.

Importantly, we should distinguish a person from their student record and from their enrollment in a particular program.
8.1 Four independent concepts
Concept	Meaning
Person	The actual individual
Institutional membership	Their platform relationship with an institution
Student record	Institution's academic identity for that person
Program enrollment	Their participation in a particular program

A person can hold two program enrollments, such as a permitted dual-degree arrangement.

They can also have different student records at different institutions.

These should not become one enormous record with fields like degree, semester, branch, batch and current_cgpa.
8.2 Student entity
A student record should hold institution-specific student identity.

StudentRecord
Attribute	Description
student_id	Internal student ID
tenant_id	Tenant
institution_id	Institution
membership_id	CAMP-02 membership
registration_number	Institutional registration number
roll_number	Student's operational roll number
admission_number	Admission reference
admission_date	Admission date
student_status	Current summarized status
created_at	Creation timestamp

The identifiers may have different uniqueness rules.

For example, roll numbers may be unique within a campus or academic cohort rather than across the whole institution.

We must define those rules according to the institution's configuration.
8.3 Student identifier architecture
Instead of storing every external ID as a separate column, create StudentIdentifier.

Examples:
- Registration number.
- Roll number.
- Admission number.
- Examination number.
- ABC ID reference.
- DigiLocker-related reference.
- Legacy ERP identifier.
Every identifier should have:
- Scheme.
- Issuer.
- Value.
- Scope.
- Validity.
- Verification state.
- Source.
Sensitive identifiers should be access-controlled and not exposed in ordinary student searches.
8.4 ProgramEnrollment
This represents the student's actual academic engagement with a program.

A curriculum update must not silently change the requirements of an existing student.
8.5 Multiple academic enrollments
Consider a student pursuing two authorized programs.
Student
    |
    +-- Program Enrollment A
    |       +-- B.Tech CSE
    |       +-- Curriculum Version 2026
    |
    +-- Program Enrollment B
            +-- Online Certificate Program
            +-- Curriculum Version 2026


Both enrollments should have independent status, calendars, course registration and academic requirements.

We can later add constraints for simultaneous program combinations, where institutional policy or applicable regulation requires them.
8.6 Cohort, batch and section are different
We must not merge these three concepts.
Entity	Purpose
Cohort	Students grouped by admission or graduation pathway
Batch	An institutional operational grouping, depending on local usage
Section	A specific instructional grouping

An institution may use "batch" to mean different things.

One college might use it for the graduation year. Another might use it for an admission group. Another might use it as a section.

I recommend the canonical model use Cohort for the admission/academic population, with an institution-specific terminology mapping for the word "batch".

Section assignment should be independent and time-bound.

A student can move from CSE-A to CSE-B without creating an entirely new student identity or program enrollment.
8.7 Cohort model

A cohort must not automatically be synonymous with an academic year.

An institution may have multiple cohorts progressing through different terms during the same academic year.

9. Student lifecycle states
A student needs an independently managed academic lifecycle.

Do not use only ACTIVE and INACTIVE.

We need to understand what the student is actually doing in the academic system.
9.1 Proposed lifecycle
ADMISSION_ACCEPTED


ENROLLED


ACTIVE

Regular academic participation

ON_LEAVE

Approved temporary absence


ACADEMICALLY_RESTRICTED

Approved restriction on progression




ACTIVE / RE-ENTERED


COMPLETED

Academic requirements met


WITHDRAWN

Enrollment discontinued




GRADUATED / EXIT AWARD ISSUED

Academic completion followed by formal award processing


This is a conceptual lifecycle, not one mandatory linear workflow. Readmission, multiple entry/exit, program transfers and withdrawals create different valid transition paths.
9.2 Important distinction: completed versus graduated
These must not be the same state.
- COMPLETED: The applicable academic requirements have been successfully met.
- GRADUATED: The awarding institution has formally conferred the qualification or approved exit award.
Award conferment and ceremony attendance are different matters. We should not make ceremony participation a prerequisite for academic completion.

Similarly, a student may receive an intermediate exit qualification while continuing or returning through an eligible pathway.
9.3 Lifecycle history
Introduce StudentLifecycleEvent.

Examples:
- Admitted.
- Enrolled.
- Academic year advanced.
- Program changed.
- Campus transferred.
- On leave.
- Re-entered.
- Withdrawn.
- Program completed.
- Degree awarded.
Every transition should be traceable to its origin, effective date, authorizing actor and applicable policy.

The current status is a projection of approved lifecycle events, not a replacement for historical records.

10. Academic registration and course enrollment
This is another area where we need to avoid mixing different academic objects.

A student being enrolled in B.Tech CSE does not mean they are automatically registered for every course in every term.

We need an independent course registration system.
10.1 Course catalogue versus course offering
Let's distinguish:

Course: The institutional academic definition.

Course version: The approved syllabus and course properties for a particular period.

Course offering: The actual delivery of that course in a particular term, possibly for one or more programs.

Course registration: A student's enrollment in that course offering.

Example:
Course:
    Database Management Systems

Course Version:
    DBMS-2026

Course Offering:
    Semester 4, 2027
    Campus North
    Faculty assignment

Sections:
    CSE-A
    CSE-B
    IT-A

Course Registrations:
    Student 1 -> CSE-A
    Student 2 -> CSE-B
    Student 3 -> IT-A


This also allows shared courses between programs.
10.2 CourseOffering
Field	Purpose
offering_id	Unique course offering
course_version_id	Approved course version
academic_term_id	Parent term
institution_id	Institution
campus_id	Delivery campus
program_scope	Relevant programs
delivery_mode	Physical, online, hybrid
capacity	Optional maximum enrollment
status	Planned, open, active, closed

10.3 CourseRegistration
Field	Purpose
registration_id	Unique record
student_enrollment_id	Program enrollment
course_offering_id	Course offering
registration_type	Regular, repeat, audit, etc.
attempt_number	Course attempt
registration_status	Lifecycle
registered_at	Timestamp
dropped_at	If withdrawn
result_reference	Published outcome reference

We must preserve previous attempts.

Suppose a student fails a subject in Semester 2 and repeats it in Semester 4.

We should have two registration/attempt records, not overwrite the old result with the new one.

11. Grade and academic result architecture
We need a system that supports multiple grading methods.

An institution may use:
- Percentage marks.
- Letter grades.
- Grade points.
- GPA.
- SGPA.
- CGPA.
- Pass/fail.
- Competency levels.
- Qualitative evaluation.
- Research or thesis assessment.
Never assume every institution uses a 10-point CGPA system.
11.1 Grading schemes
Create GradingScheme and GradingSchemeVersion.

Example:
Grade	Grade point	Interpretation
O	10	Outstanding
A+	9	Excellent
A	8	Very good
B+	7	Good
B	6	Above average
C	5	Average
F	0	Fail

These values are illustrative, not a universal Indian grading standard.

The grading scheme should contain:
- Marks intervals, where applicable.
- Grade labels.
- Grade points, where applicable.
- Pass/fail classification.
- Award status.
- Credit eligibility.
- Grade replacement/attempt rules.
- Rounding policies.
- Version and effective dates.
11.2 Academic result snapshots
CAMP-08 owns individual result publications.

CAMP-03 should maintain a reference or derived academic record containing:
- Student.
- Course attempt.
- Published result ID.
- Grade/marks.
- Credits awarded.
- Grading scheme version.
- Publication timestamp.
- Correction/revision reference.
We should retain the exact result version used when a progression decision was made.

For example, a revised result should trigger a new academic evaluation, but historical evaluations must continue to show what information was used when they were originally calculated.
11.3 GPA calculations
We should configure the formula.

For a weighted grade-point calculation:
\[
GPA = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i G_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i}
\]

Where:
- \(C_i\) is the relevant credit value.
- \(G_i\) is the applicable grade point.
But even this formula may vary by institutional grading policy, exclusions, repeated attempts, audit courses and rounding rules.

Therefore, implement configurable calculation strategies.

Store:
- Raw source results.
- Grading scheme.
- Calculation policy.
- Calculated value.
- Calculation timestamp.
- Input record references.
Never store only cgpa = 8.2 without its calculation lineage.

12. Academic progression and rule engine
This is the brain of the student lifecycle.

It determines whether a student can move forward through the academic program.

We should not embed progression rules in frontend logic or hardcode them in individual API endpoints.

Use a configurable academic rules engine, building on our shared policy architecture.
12.1 Types of academic rules
Rule	Example
Admission	Accepted qualification and prerequisites
Course registration	Required prior courses
Semester progression	Minimum accumulated credits
Backlog	Permitted number of failed courses
Academic standing	Minimum CGPA
Re-registration	Conditions for repeating a course
Completion	All mandatory program requirements satisfied
Credit transfer	Conditions for external recognition
Exit award	Requirements for an intermediate award
Maximum duration	Maximum permitted completion period

An institution may have different policies for different programs, cohorts and academic years.
12.2 Progression evaluation pipeline
Academic progression engine
01. Trigger

End of term, result publication, transfer request or student re-entry


02. Academic context

Student enrollment, program version, cohort, term and previous progression


03. Academic facts

Published results, credit ledger, course attempts and verified attendance aggregates where applicable


04. Applicable policy resolution

Program, curriculum, cohort and institutional rule versions


05. Deterministic rule evaluation

Credits, failed courses, prerequisites, duration and required outcomes


Eligible
Progression permitted


Blocked
Requirement not met


Review
Decision required




06. Decision and history

Decision record, evidence, policy versions, approvals and next action


The engine should distinguish:
- Requirement passed.
- Requirement not passed.
- Requirement unknown.
- Requirement exempted.
- Requirement awaiting authoritative data.
Missing results, for example, should not automatically be treated as failures.
12.3 Progression decisions
Possible outcomes:
Decision	Meaning
ELIGIBLE	All required conditions are met
NOT_ELIGIBLE	One or more mandatory conditions are not met
REVIEW_REQUIRED	An authorized human decision is necessary
INCOMPLETE_DATA	Required academic facts are unavailable
EXEMPTED	An approved exception applies

A decision should contain:
- Student enrollment.
- Academic period.
- Applicable curriculum version.
- Policy version.
- Evaluated facts.
- Failed conditions.
- Approved exemptions.
- Decision timestamp.
- Reviewer information, if applicable.
12.4 Never conflate academic progression with placement eligibility
This is especially important for our broader platform.

A student may:
- Be academically active but not placement eligible.
- Have backlogs but still be allowed to register for the next term.
- Have completed all requirements but await formal graduation.
- Be graduated but not eligible for a particular company drive.
Academic progression and placement eligibility must be independent decision systems.

CAMP-09 should consume academic facts from CAMP-03 rather than reimplementing academic rules.


13. Student mobility, transfer and re-entry
A modern academic system cannot assume that a student always studies one program at one institution from beginning to end.

We need to support mobility.

Possible situations:
- Transfer between campuses.
- Transfer between programs.
- Transfer between institutions.
- Change of specialization.
- Multiple entry and exit.
- Recognition of prior learning.
- Credit transfer.
- Readmission after a break.
- Migration from one academic framework to another.
13.1 Internal transfer
Suppose a student changes from B.Tech ECE to B.Tech CSE.

We should not simply update:
student.department_id = CSE


Instead:
Current Program Enrollment
        |
Transfer Request
        |
Academic Equivalence Evaluation
        |
Credit Mapping
        |
Approval
        |
Close/transition old program enrollment
        |
Create or transition new enrollment
        |
Assign applicable curriculum version
        |
Re-evaluate academic standing
        |
Record transition history


A transfer can preserve credits, but the receiving program may have different requirements.

Therefore, transfer approval and credit recognition are separate decisions.
13.2 External institutional transfer
This should use an external-credit recognition workflow.

The receiving institution needs to evaluate:
- Issuing institution.
- Course equivalence.
- Credit value.
- Learning outcomes.
- Academic level.
- Grading scheme.
- Completion and evidence.
- Applicable validity or expiry conditions.
- Approval authority.
We should not automatically transfer an external CGPA into the receiving institution's CGPA.

The receiving institution may recognize the credits without treating the original grade points as equivalent.
13.3 Multiple entry and exit
A student may complete a certain stage, receive an approved qualification and later return for further study.

Our model should represent this as an award/qualification event associated with a program enrollment, rather than forcing the student enrollment into a single irreversible graduation status.

For example:
Bachelors Program Enrollment
        |
First exit requirement satisfied
        |
Approved intermediate qualification
        |
Program exit recorded
        |
Later readmission / re-entry
        |
Further credit and curriculum evaluation
        |
Higher qualification completed


The applicable rules must be institution- and framework-specific.
13.4 Academic equivalence engine
This is a separate capability worth designing.

It compares:
- Course identities.
- Learning outcomes.
- Credit values.
- Academic levels.
- Assessment structures.
- Grading systems.
- Relevant approval rules.
An AI model may assist with course-description similarity or outcome comparison, but it must not independently grant academic credit.

Final credit recognition must be governed by authorized institutional decisions.

17. Database integrity and transaction rules
We need to define the critical invariants before implementation.
Rule	Why
A program offering belongs to one tenant and institution	Isolation
A student enrollment follows an approved program version	Curriculum integrity
A course registration belongs to a valid course offering	Referential integrity
Course registration must reference an appropriate student enrollment	Academic integrity
Repeat attempts remain distinct	History
A published curriculum cannot be silently modified	Version integrity
Credit awards have evidence and an issuing authority	Traceability
Credit corrections use reversal/adjustment entries	Ledger integrity
External credit must be recognized before counting toward requirements	Academic governance
Progression decisions reference the policy version used	Reproducibility
Graduate status requires formal award evidence	Credential integrity

Some recommended database constraints
- Unique institution-scoped program codes.
- Unique course codes within the applicable catalogue scope.
- No overlapping active program-version effective periods for the same intended cohort assignment.
- Unique course registration for a given student enrollment and offering where duplicate registration is prohibited.
- Non-negative credit values unless an explicit reversal transaction is being recorded.
- Composite tenant-aware foreign keys.
- Valid course attempt sequences.
- Immutable published result references.
The database should enforce what can be expressed as constraints. Complex academic decisions should be handled by the domain and rules engine.

18. Difficult academic edge cases

18.1 The most important exception-handling rule
Missing or conflicting academic data should not automatically become a failure.

For example:
- Missing semester result ≠ failed semester.
- Missing transfer documentation ≠ zero credit.
- Unmapped external course ≠ accepted equivalence.
- Missing attendance feed ≠ attendance shortage.
- Unknown grading conversion ≠ rejected academic performance.
The system should distinguish NOT_MET, UNKNOWN, PENDING, EXEMPTED and REQUIRES_REVIEW.

That distinction is fundamental to fair and reliable academic decisions.

21. Scale and performance architecture
The academic domain is significantly more data-intensive than CAMP-01.

A university can have tens of thousands of students, hundreds of programs, thousands of courses, millions of historical course registrations and a substantial amount of academic history.

We need to separate transactional work from heavy reporting.
21.1 Workload characteristics
Workload	Characteristics
Student profile lookup	Read-heavy
Academic history	Read-heavy, potentially large
Course registration	Burst writes
Result publication	High-volume writes
Credit-ledger updates	Transactional
Progression recalculation	Background/batch
Transcript generation	Background
Student data import	Large batch
Department reporting	Analytical
Placement eligibility refresh	Event-driven/background

21.2 Scaling strategy
I recommend:
- PostgreSQL for canonical academic records.
- Asynchronous workers for bulk import and recalculation.
- Transactional outbox for reliable event publication.
- Tenant-aware indexes and partitioning only when measured workloads require it.
- Versioned read models for student dashboards.
- Materialized aggregates or analytical warehouse for departmental reporting.
- Object storage for transcripts and formal documents.
- Dedicated search infrastructure for complex discovery when needed.
One important optimization:

Do not recalculate the complete CGPA, all credits, every progression rule and every student's academic history whenever one result changes.

Use incremental projections, but retain the ability to perform full reconciliation against authoritative records.

27. Five architectural invariants I particularly want to preserve
These will affect almost every future module:
1. No hardcoded institutional assumptions. A university's academic structure, calendars, grading rules and workflows must be configurable.
2. No destruction of history. Transfers, curriculum changes, grade corrections and institutional restructuring must preserve previous states.
3. No duplicated ownership. Each domain has one authoritative owner for its information.
4. No unexplained decisions. Eligibility, progression, grading and authorization decisions must be traceable to their inputs and policy versions.
5. No AI replacing institutional authority. AI can recommend, classify and identify anomalies, but formal academic decisions remain governed by authorized processes.
28. What comes immediately after this?
I suggest we don't rush into CAMP-04 yet.

Before expanding the functional domains, let's turn CAMP-01, CAMP-02 and CAMP-03 into an implementation-ready foundation.

This will also let us identify any contradictions between the three domain designs before developers start implementing them.

One additional architectural goal: I want every future campus module to plug into this foundation through defined contracts, without having to reinvent identity, authorization, institutional hierarchy, academic records or workflow handling.

That's how we make the platform extensible while maintaining engineering discipline.
